# 006 · BROADCAST — the familiar, on stage

**Thesis.** On stream, the audience can't hear your monitor mix and can't read your
DAW — but they can watch a creature *react*. BROADCAST makes the act of making music
legible as drama: Moshi dances the beat center-stage, runs hot and ON-AIR when a take
is live, lays an egg into the clutch when a take lands, and the pit (chat) feeds it
VIBE/REDO votes. Deeds roll on a ticker like sports stats. One toggle drops the stage
to OBS-transparent for compositing over gameplay or a camera.

**The fiction, literally.** A familiar performs for the crowd on its host's behalf.
The audience isn't watching software; they're watching the symbiote work — which is
exactly the brand's joke played outward.

**The two non-negotiables** (now for the *audience* as much as the artist):
- *Recording:* ON-AIR ember + the creature visibly hot + "TAKE LIVE" — broadcast-grade
  unambiguity, since a streamer's worst failure is not knowing they're live.
- *Position:* the halo ring around Moshi, one revolution per loop, ticks at sections —
  readable at 240p in a stream thumbnail.

**Feel.** Watchable. The vtuber-asset quality the earlier research predicted falls out
for free; eggs and the pit give chat something to *do*.

**Risks.** Tone management — it must hype the artist without turning the session into
content-brain. The votes must stay advisory (the artist is always the host).

**Steal even if killed.** The halo-as-position (works in every direction); take-eggs as
session bookkeeping; the OBS-transparent stage as a first-class product mode.
