## The movement

**Seeded Geology** holds that a piece of music is a geological event: pressure
applied over time to raw matter, leaving a stone that could only have formed from
*that* pressure, in *that* order, at *that* heat. The algorithm does not decorate
the music — it is the sediment the music leaves behind. Every input is a force
(spectral mass, midrange structure, top-end light, the violence of the moment), and
the body that accretes is the proof that those forces happened.

Its randomness is never loose. Every stochastic choice flows from a seed, and every
seed flows from the *work itself* — the arrangement hash, the section index, the
clips the musician actually placed. Two songs can never grow the same stone; one
song always regrows its own. This is the Art Blocks covenant applied to sound:
reproducibility is not a technical nicety, it is what makes the artifact *theirs*.
The bounded ranges around every derived parameter are the product of painstaking
calibration — wide enough that no two stones feel related, narrow enough that none
is ever ugly. Controlled chaos, refined until the dice can only roll beauty.

Continuity is sacred. Matter does not teleport: seeded states crossfade between
fixed endpoints, lobes glide as sections turn, growth accretes at the speed of
sediment rather than the speed of the framebuffer. The cardinal sin — deriving
noise from a blended value, so that hidden wraps spray discontinuities across a
transition — is named here so it is never committed again. A master-level
implementation is recognizable precisely by what never happens on screen.

The render register is part of the philosophy, not a post effect: quarter-resolution
buffers, ordered Bayer dither, three light bands, faceted normals, animation on
twos. These are not nostalgia stickers; they are a constraint system that makes
soft-gradient slop *unrepresentable*. Beauty must be earned inside a coarse grid —
the same way a 2002 console earned it — and the polychrome fringe is the single,
deliberate exception: a reward that exists only when the music's top end is full.

And one quiet thing, for those who know: the stone smiles back. The organism's
lobed silhouette is the same smin grammar as the small creature in the corner —
parent and child of one body of code. The interface is a terrarium; everything in
it is alive, seeded, and listening.
