Make it. Mosh it.

Mosh is a music studio for your Mac with a creature living in the corner. Drop in a beat, record a vocal, and tell Moshi what you want changed.

Get the playtest build See what’s inside

For Apple Silicon Macs on macOS 11 or later.

Open it and start a song.

Mosh is a native Mac app. The drum kit, the synth, the tuner and the mix tools are already inside when you open it.

Start with a beat

One button drops a drum beat onto the built-in kit. Add a MIDI track, pick a synth preset, and write the 808 underneath it.

Record the vocal

Arm a track, set a count-in, and sing. Takes land on the timeline as clips you can cut, double and stack into a hook.

Tune it and mix it

Mosh AutoTune, a compressor and a four-band EQ ship with the app. Your own VST3 and AU plugins load beside them and open in their own windows.

Re-Imagine a take

Select a clip, describe where it should go, and Mosh renders a new version of that take on your own Mac. Audition it, then keep it or throw it away.

Ask Moshi in plain words.

Moshi makes the change with the same commands your own clicks use, so it shows up in History and one undo takes it back.

Lead → Reverb send −12 → −9 dB (+3 dB by default)

Moshi hangs around until you ask, goes wide-eyed while the mic is live, laughs when a change lands, and curls into a ball while you record so nothing distracts the take.

Make a song with a friend.

Start a session, send a room code, and you are both inside the same project, each on your own Mac.

One track each

Each of you takes a track, and a small lock shows who is editing what. The song syncs between you. Your voices don’t, so keep a call open on the side.

Your phone as a pad

Still early. Scan a code with the iPhone camera and a controller for the session opens in Safari, with no app to install.
